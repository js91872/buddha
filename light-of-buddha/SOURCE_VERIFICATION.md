# Source verification report

Research access date: 23 October 2026.

All thirty selected narrative or teaching sources were opened through the available web research service as live English primary-text translations. `research/source-verification.csv` records the exact canonical identifier, PTS starting page, passage cues, source URL, language, classification, rights limitations and factual caveats for every story. Individual readable dossiers are in `research/story-sources/01.md` through `30.md`.

This verifies that the selected teachings and narrative anchors occur in the cited canonical tradition. It does **not** establish that the events are independently proven history, certify every detail of the literary retellings, or constitute human Buddhist scholarly approval. A canonical discourse can include legends, supernatural events and hypothetical illustrations. These classifications overlap: “canonical” describes textual status, not modern historical certainty.

## Actual access and limitations

- Twenty-eight texts were opened at Access to Insight. The MN21 page is explicitly an excerpt; it includes Vedehika/Kali and the speech/saw teaching used here.
- SN3.13 and SN42.7 were opened on the translator’s official Dhammatalks site after guessed ATI paths failed. No failed URL is represented as verified.
- The SuttaCentral MN26 and licensing frontend pages returned a JavaScript/unsupported-browser shell. Their text was **not** inspected through that frontend. SuttaCentral comparison links are supplied for readers, not falsely marked as individually checked Pali editions.
- The official SuttaCentral Bilara repository license was found through indexed search and opened; it specifies CC0 for supported Bilara translations. This is not a blanket license for every translation on SuttaCentral.
- No complete Pali manuscript collation, Sanskrit/Chinese/Tibetan parallel collation, archaeological authentication or human philological review was performed. Cross-tradition context is documented separately.
- Tool access returned indexed pages and excerpts, not an independently archived complete edition. No source-file hash is asserted. The recorded date identifies the research session rather than a publisher-guaranteed immutable snapshot.

## Copyright boundary

ATI Thanissaro Bhikkhu pages identify modern English translations as Creative Commons Attribution–NonCommercial 4.0. These were consulted for identifying ancient narrative and teaching content; their English sentences must not be reused commercially. The DN16 Vajira/Story translation is also a modern protected work consulted for research. Sujato’s supported Bilara translations are CC0, but use still merits attribution. The book must describe its prose as original literary retellings, not newly discovered verbatim Buddha speech or a certified direct translation from Pali.

## Required interpretive distinctions

MN63’s wounded man, MN22’s raft, SN36.6’s arrows and SN47.19’s acrobats are teaching illustrations. Original story settings and inner thoughts are literary additions. Brahma in SN6.1, Mara in SN5.2, the supernormal pursuit in MN86 and the heaven journey in Ud3.2 are traditional canonical supernatural narratives. They should not be silently secularised into established historical events. AN5.57 and MN131 chiefly contain instruction, so invented narrative frames require explicit disclosure.

SN42.7 concerns how teaching is offered to different audiences; it is not originally about dividing monetary gifts. SN3.14 is the first battle; a reversal belongs to SN3.15 and needs its own citation. MN61’s mirror is an analogy and need not be a physical object. No date for the Buddha’s death, identification of the final meal, or precise modern conversion of the king’s rice measures is asserted.

## Editorial handoff

The CSV’s “verification status” deliberately distinguishes source identification from manuscript-to-source review. Independent draft review, quotation review, accessibility checks and final human approval must be documented by the relevant production reports; this report cannot substitute for them.
