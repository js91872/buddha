# Scholarly contextual checks

Accessed through the web research service, 23 October 2026. The accessible article introductions and contextual passages below were read; no claim to have surveyed their entire bibliographies or to have independently collated all parallels is made.

1. Bhikkhu Anālayo, “The Buddha’s Pre-awakening Practices and Their Mindful Transformation,” *Mindfulness* 12 (2021): 1892–1898. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12671-021-01646-0

The discussion of the quest emphasises that early discourses provide scattered instructional recollections rather than a continuous biography. It supports keeping the search stories distinct and avoiding later narrative additions. Its treatment of moderation also places the king’s eating instruction within a wider practice context. These observations support the editorial framing; the article’s English translations and comparative arguments have not been incorporated as story dialogue.

2. Bhikkhu Anālayo, “The Buddha’s Awakening,” *Mindfulness* 12 (2021): 2141–2148. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12671-021-01649-x

The introduction and comparative discussion acknowledge differences between early textual accounts and the limits of historical interpretation. This supports edition-specific source notes and avoiding claims that a literary synthesis reproduces an undisputed historical transcript. The present collection does not resolve the textual-development questions raised in that study.

3. “Buddha,” *Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, sections “Dates” and “Sources.” https://iep.utm.edu/buddha/

The accessible scholarly overview records chronological disagreement and distinguishes early textual witnesses from the historical Buddha’s verbatim speech. Its overview supports avoiding a precise death date and distinguishing canonical attribution from established historicity. Some specific historical judgements in any encyclopaedia remain debatable; they are not treated here as blanket certification.
