## Findings: first four files reviewed

1. **The Search No Teacher Could Finish / MN26:** Follows departure with unwilling parents, Alara’s sphere of nothingness, Rama-associated attainment in Uddaka’s community, departure and Uruvela. Does not import the nighttime palace escape, horse or charioteer. Clearly distinguishes the teaching associated with Rama rather than stating that Uddaka himself attained it. Literary interpretation of the difficulty of leaving success is marked by general reconstruction note. No substantive source error identified.
2. **The Shade He Remembered / MN36:** Distinguishes austerities from awakening, childhood rose-apple memory from Bodhi tree, nourishing food from indulgence. Does not insert Sujata into this discourse. Five companions leave after food is resumed, consistent with the source. Former-life and rebirth knowledge are identified as tradition’s claims. No substantive source error identified.
3. **The Cowherd of the Mind / MN19:** Correct distinction between harmful and harmless thought groups; cattle/crops images are explicitly similes. Retains the important limit that even wholesome discursive thought can tire body and disturb mind. Modern practical application is editorial commentary rather than a fabricated canonical instruction. No substantive source error identified.
4. **When the Forest Made a Sound / MN4:** Retains conduct inventory, fear-associated shrines, and remaining in the posture in which fear arose. Source note and reflection correctly avoid implying anxious people are morally at fault or readers should remain in unsafe places. Bird/twig/wind imagery is scene reconstruction rather than claimed historical observation. No substantive source error identified.

Narrative assessment: distinct openings and imagery; original indirect discourse; no imported direct Buddha quotations identified. These early practice accounts are retrospective sources and need not represent a single continuous dated chronology; the current framing generally makes that clear.

## Findings: remaining six files reviewed

5. **The Lotuses Not Yet Open / SN6.1:** Correct Goatherd’s Banyan near Neranjara rather than conflating scene with the Bodhi tree. Brahma Sahampati is explicitly a celestial figure in a supernatural canonical narrative. Differing capacities and lotus comparison fit the discourse; expansion into patience is editorial. No substantive source error identified.
6. **A Way Between the Extremes / SN56.11:** Correct Isipatana/five listeners, two extremes, Noble Eightfold Path, Four Noble Truths with their distinct tasks, and Kondanna’s initial breakthrough. Does not reduce middle way to generic moderation. Celestial announcement/radiance is classified as sacred motif. No substantive source error identified.
7. **What the Hand Cannot Hold / SN22.59:** Five aggregates, affliction/lack of sovereign control, impermanence, disidentification and liberation are coherent with the source. Avoids treating not-self as nihilism or lack of care. The modern mood-label exercise is expressly modest, not the whole teaching. No substantive source error identified; specialist doctrinal review remains advisable for this subtle subject.
8. **The Mirror Before the Deed / MN61:** Welcome, foot-water vessel and truthfulness lesson fit source. Mirror is a comparison, not asserted physical prop. Before/during/after reflection and distinction between disclosure for bodily/verbal acts versus inward correction for harmful mental acts are retained. No triggering lie is invented. No substantive source error identified.
9. **The Question That Could Wait / MN63:** Malunkyaputta’s conditional ultimatum, absence of prior promise and hypothetical poisoned-arrow patient are clearly distinguished. Does not depict Buddha as actual treating physician, reject science or promise answers to all philosophical questions. The source’s ten speculative positions are compressed as examples rather than exhaustively enumerated. No substantive source error identified.
10. **A Calmness Put to the Test / MN21:** Vedehika and enslaved Kali belong to Buddha’s teaching story; the successive lateness, anger, blow and changed reputation fit the source. Nonhatred and saw are training/hypothetical, not actual torture of Buddha. Explicitly recognises unequal power and avoids blaming the injured woman or advising dangerous submission. No substantive source error identified.
