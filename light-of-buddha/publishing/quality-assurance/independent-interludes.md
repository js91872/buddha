# Independent AI review of thirteen interludes

Reviewed 23 October 2026 by the stories11–20 agent, distinct from the interlude drafting/verification agent. This is a bounded independent AI comparison, not human Pali philological approval or certification of translation quality.

## Actual review

Read all thirteen actual numbered manuscript/verses-and-chants files and verification.md. Opened the ten cited Ancient Buddhist Texts original-language Dhammapada chapter pages through the web tool and the Sutta Friends Khp1 page. Used find/reopened passages to read the selected lines rather than treating successful URL access as textual verification. No shell network requests were made. Compared the manuscript Pali spellings against the displayed ancient verses, allowing the explicitly declared nasal, compound, capitalisation and punctuation regularisation. Reviewed the original English meanings for substantial omissions, reversed meanings, misleading claims and tradition misattribution.

## Findings by selection

| Interlude | Source | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Khp1, first cycle | Three Pali refuge lines match after normalising nasal and saraṇa spelling. Correctly identifies excerpt from a three-cycle recitation. “Turn for refuge” is an intelligible rendering of gacchāmi in this formula. No invented mantra attribution. |
| 2 | Dhp1 | Pali matches selected edition after declared word-division normalisation, including dukkhamanveti. English preserves corrupted mind, speech/action and following suffering. The interpretation of dhammā as “these experiences” is not uniquely required, but is a defensible contextual rendering. Context correctly avoids claiming thought manufactures all external events. |
| 3 | Dhp21 | Pali matches. Meaning preserves heedfulness/heedlessness and Deathless/death contrast. Context explicitly prevents a promise of bodily immortality. |
| 4 | Dhp35 | Pali matches. Difficult restraint, quick movement, alighting at will, training and happiness are all represented. Context avoids treating thoughts as hated enemies. |
| 5 | Dhp5 | Pali matches. Non-hostility, rather than revenge or compulsory passivity, is correctly retained. “Enduring principle” is a restrained rendering of sanantano dhammo. |
| 6 | Dhp50 | Pali matches. Own actions/omissions versus others’ faults is correctly represented. Context does not silence recognition of injustice. |
| 7 | Dhp81 | Pali matches the main reading samiñjanti rather than the listed Thai variant sammiñjanti. Rock, wind, wise and praise/blame are correct. Useful criticism is not dismissed. |
| 8 | Dhp103 | Pali matches the main reading ekañca jeyya attānaṃ, with declared hyphen normalisation. Numerical contrast and self-mastery are retained. English “greater victor” is comparative in tone; “greatest victor” would mirror uttamo’s superlative more literally, but current meaning does not reverse or materially distort the contrast. |
| 9 | Dhp160 | Pali matches. Guardian/protection rendering of nātha is defensible. Context correctly distinguishes conventional self-reference from an eternal-soul assertion and does not reject mutual care. |
| 10 | Dhp183 | Pali matches with regularised spacing and nasal. The source marks a metrical loss in buddhāna’; manuscript declares its contracted form. English retains avoidance of harm, cultivation of skilful qualities, own-mind purification and plural Buddhas. No singular-Gautama misattribution. |
| 11 | Dhp184 | Pali matches main edition. Endurance, supreme nibbana and non-injury criteria are represented. The English negative in the final line makes the contextual force clear despite the Pali stanza’s compressed syntax. Context does not license teacher abuse. |
| 12 | Dhp204 | Pali matches including ārogya, santuṭṭhi, vissāsa and nibbāna. Health/gain, contentment/wealth, trust/kinship and nibbana/happiness correspond. “Trust is the finest kinship” is interpretive rather than word-for-word, but retains the comparison. No medical promise added. |
| 13 | Dhp277 | Pali matches the edition’s nibbindatī reading; variant nibbindati is already documented. Wisdom, conditioned formations’ impermanence, disenchantment with suffering and purification are retained. Does not replace saṅkhārā with an unqualified assertion that every possible thing is conditioned. |

## Result and limits

No substantial spelling mismatch, reversed meaning, fabricated canonical reference, later mantra presented as Gautama’s speech, or dangerous bodily-immortality claim was identified. No manuscript alteration was made. The Dhp103 comparative/superlative nuance is an optional precision improvement rather than a release-blocking semantic error.

These are all Pali canonical selections in a Theravada textual frame. They do not represent the full range of Mahayana or Vajrayana liturgy, nor do they claim to do so. Modern meanings are original editorial renderings and still require qualified human Pali review. Visual inspection of rendered diacritics in EPUB/PDF is a separate production check; this text comparison does not establish font coverage in the released artifacts.
