Date: 23 October 2026. Review performed by an AI drafting agent; no human scholarly or copyediting approval is implied.

All ten narratives contain 500–750 words before the Wisdom to Remember section. Initial-draft whitespace counts: 11:534; 12:527; 13:535; 14:554; 15:545; 16:558; 17:573; 18:551; 19:538; 20:554. See editorial-11-20.md for current counts and corrections after a second pass.

Each story includes a distinct opening, narrative turn, brief wisdom, practical reflection and source note. Canonical speech is reported indirectly; no sentence is labelled a verbatim Buddha quotation. Sensory and transitional staging is expressly identified as literary reconstruction. The ancient canonical episode is the retelling basis; modern translations were consulted for reference rather than reproduced.

## Editorial decisions and caveats

- MN22 raft is clearly a hypothetical teaching simile; no historical traveller is invented.
- SN7.2 non-retaliation does not require remaining in danger or tolerating abuse.
- SN7.11 steaming milk-rice remains explicitly supernatural; teaching is not sold for alms.
- AN3.65 is not presented as permission to believe whatever feels right. Wise counsel and consequences remain essential.
- MN58 choking infant is hypothetical. The later commentary's claim about Abhaya pinching the child is excluded.
- MN86 does not erase victims, imply immunity from law, or offer childbirth ritual as medicine. Miraculous pursuit is labelled supernatural.
- MN93 hearth is a hypothetical comparison; any illustration should not claim an actual canonical hearth scene. Ancient social categories do not precisely equal every later caste institution.
- MN140 includes Pukkusati's sudden death and canonical non-returning declaration; later royal biography is excluded.
- SN5.2 identifies Mara as the canonical adversary; a psychological reading is identified as interpretation, not substituted as fact.
- SN3.13 identifies Sudassana as young brahmin and uses no exact modern dietary prescription. Later Uttara versions are distinguished.
