# Editorial review — working record

This project was researched, drafted, edited and checked by AI agents. No Buddhist scholar, professional human editor, proofreader or publisher has reviewed or endorsed it. The requested professional disciplines are treated as review responsibilities, not invented credentials.

## Editorial decisions

The 30-source outline is recorded in manuscript/editorial-outline.md. A source-led Pali focus was chosen rather than mixing anonymous modern anecdotes into a supposed historical biography. The four thematic parts retain the requested exact names. Stories are original retellings, with indirect dialogue and separate Wisdom to Remember, practical reflection and source notes. Canonical inclusion is distinguished from historical verification. Sensory staging, symbolic illustrations and supernatural events are identified.

The book uses international English and retains readable names in running prose, with diacritics in source notes, glossary and Pali interludes. The compiler credit remains exactly “Compiled by Jaspal Singh”; no personal biography has been invented.

## Recorded substantive corrections

- Angulimala's childbirth blessing, attainment and later assault were reordered to match MN86. The story preserves accountability and victims' suffering.
- Abhaya's infant is described lying on his lap rather than sitting independently.
- The early seeking accounts avoid importing a midnight horse departure, named palace attendants or the later four-sights narrative.
- The raft, poisoned arrow, second arrow, fire and lute scenes are treated as teaching similes or interpretive visualisations.
- Soma's encounter is her own response to Mara, not an occasion on which Buddha rescues her.
- SN3.14's defeated Pasenadi is distinguished from the separate capture-and-release episode of SN3.15.
- The final meal is not assigned an established modern ingredient or a medical diagnosis.

## Evidence and limits

See publishing/quality-assurance/ for actual story review reports and mechanical word/section/duplicate checks. Automated word counts do not establish literary merit. Source verification is recorded separately and should not be inferred merely from a valid-looking link. All small verse renderings must be reviewed as translations, not labelled human-translated or quotations from a modern translator.

Before publication, a qualified human Buddhist reader should review doctrinal nuance and Pali meanings; a literary editor should review pacing, emotional depth and repetition across the complete book. A final proofreader should check the actual EPUB and PDF, not only the editable manuscript. These remain approval requirements; no completed human review is claimed.
